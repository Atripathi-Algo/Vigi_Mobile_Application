K app audit: usability, feature map and visual
design
Oct 28, 2026  · ​@AAClaude4
Summary
K's best idea, an assistant that actually changes your data and lets you undo it, is buried
under a control-room layout and a military vocabulary. The fix is less, not more: make the
privacy claims true, cut 13 navigation destinations to 4 tabs plus a K button, use plain
names, then add motion that answers what the user just did.
The five changes that matter most, in order:
1. Make the privacy story true. Turn off Android auto-backup, run speech recognition on
the phone, and remove the false "Encrypted" label. These block any public release.
2. Collapse the navigation. Four tabs (Today, Plan, Track, Money) and K as a floating
button on every screen. Garage, journal and screen time become optional modules.
3. Rename for humans. Objectives become Tasks, Protocols become Habits, Vault
becomes Money, Loadout becomes Settings. Keep the Thor feel in visuals and sound,
not in words people must learn.
4. Make K the hero. A smaller orb, context-aware suggestion chips, a Confirm button
instead of typing "yes", and visible on-device AI answers.
5. Give it a motion system. Every tab and sub-tab currently switches with no transition.
Add shared-axis transitions, glass on floating layers only, spark bursts on completion
and a shader-lit 3D emblem.
Keep what already works: the rules-first brain with Undo and delete confirmation, the
colour tokens, Chakra Petch and IBM Plex type, short lightning tied to actions, and the
respect for reduced-motion settings.
What was reviewed
The audit covers the repo Vigi_Mobile_Application at commit 5d8ccbe , 11 screenshots
with demo data, and a 40-second emulator recording of K handling four commands.

Signs of over-engineering
A dictionary to learn. Ops, Objectives, Threat, Protocols, Vitals, Vault, Ledger, Field log,
Loadout, Uplink, Deep ops and Sitrep all name ordinary things.
Shouting labels. The screens hard-code about 166 different all-caps strings in tracked
monospace, so every card and row reads with the same weight.
Three primary buttons at once. The K tab, the add button and the mic are all bright
blue octagons on the chat and Ops screens.
A score nobody can read. Readiness mixes tasks, habits, focus, water and sleep
( store.dart:1211 ), but the Home card shows only three of those inputs. In the
screenshot, water alone produces the 10.
An 8-step first run. Welcome, profile, Google, restore, brain, permissions, voice and
ready, before the user has done anything useful.

Proposed navigation
Four tabs and a floating K button cover every feature worth keeping. Each tab answers
one everyday question: what's happening today, what's planned, how am I doing, and
where's my money.
Grey items are new or optional. Modules the user unticks in setup (bike, journal, money)
disappear from their tab, so a student who only wants tasks and habits sees two short
tabs, not thirteen screens.
Ease of use
A new user should be able to add a task by voice within 60 seconds of installing. Today the
first run has 8 steps and every screen speaks in code names.

First run: 8 steps down to 4
1. Name. One field, used in greetings.
2. What should K help with? Tick modules: tasks, habits, money, health, bike. Unticked
modules stay hidden, which removes most of the clutter for most people.
3. One guided command. "Try saying: remind me to call Mom at 6 pm." The user sees K
act, with Undo, in the first minute.
4. Done. Land on Today.
Ask for permissions when a feature first needs them: the mic on the first mic tap, Health
Connect when Health is first opened, exact alarms when the first timed reminder is set.
Offer the AI model download at the first open question, showing its size.
Plain names
The theme can keep its voice in small places, such as the "K // INTEL" card header and K's
dry replies. Navigation labels and buttons should use words people already know.

=== PAGE 10 ===
What works
A real token system. Colours, type and shapes live in lib/vigil/theme , and screens
use them consistently.
A recognisable identity. Square cards with corner brackets, chamfered buttons, the
octagon and signal blue on deep navy read as one product.
Readable colour contrast. Even the faintest text colour ( #7A8FB0 ) reaches 5.6:1 on
cards, above the 4.5:1 accessibility minimum.
Good type pairing. Chakra Petch for headings and IBM Plex for body text suit the
theme.
Responsible effects. Lightning is short, tied to actions, and skipped when the phone
asks for reduced motion.
What holds it back
No depth. Card surface #0C1424  is only 1.07:1 against the background #060B16 , and
the raised surface is 1.08:1 against the card. Layers are told apart by thin borders alone,
which is why screens feel flat.
Tiny, shouting labels. Labels are 10–11 px uppercase monospace with wide tracking.
They are readable in contrast but slow to scan, and there are dozens per screen.
Every card looks equally important. The readiness card, an empty bills block and a
settings row share the same border, bracket and caps header.
Status colour goes unused. "Tyre · 14 days" and "Chain · 142 km" get the same grey
whether they are due tomorrow or next year. Warning amber and critical red exist in
the tokens but rarely appear.
Competing bright shapes. Two or three filled blue octagons on one screen pull the eye
away from content.
Grid everywhere. The HUD grid behind every screen adds texture but also noise
behind text. Keep it on Today and K, and remove it from lists and forms.

=== PAGE 11 ===
Motion, 3D, sparks, lightning and glass
Yes, the design can feel much more alive, and the gap is mostly motion, not effects. Every
tab and sub-tab switches with NoTransitionPage  ( router.dart:25 ), only three implicit
animations exist (all in controls.dart ), and the lightning strike is used on five screens.
Between strikes the app is static.
Principles
Motion answers an action. A tap, a completed task or a K reply earns movement.
Nothing animates on a loop except the K orb.
One ambient effect at a time. The orb or the dawn-briefing storm, never both on
screen.
Glass only where things float. Blur costs GPU time per frame, so use it on the nav bar,
sheets and dialogs, never on list items.
Lightning is a reward. Big strikes for streak milestones and a cleared day; small sparks
for single ticks.
Safe by default. Flashes stay under 300 ms and never exceed three per second (the
WCAG 2.3.1 seizure threshold). Everything respects the system reduce-motion setting.
Frame budget. 16.7 ms per frame at 60 Hz and 8.3 ms at 120 Hz on the target phone (an
Oppo Reno 8). Profile every effect in release mode with the Flutter DevTools
performance view.

Roadmap
Fix trust in week 1, simplify over the next three weeks, and only then add the new feel and
features. Animation on top of a confusing layout makes the confusion louder.
Each green diamond is a gate: the next phase starts only when its check passes. Phase 2
needs a profiling pass on the Oppo Reno 8 in release mode before the effects ship.
